Project 01 | Version 01

V1 - Initial Build

STRYKER V1

Manual-control 6 DOF robotic arm prototype built to validate the mechanical, electrical, and actuation foundation for later autonomous operation.

DOF

6 (yaw, shoulder, elbow, wrist x2, gripper)

Fabrication

FDM 3D printed, PLA

Control

ESP32 + dedicated servo driver

Power

6V, bulk capacitors for transient suppression

Goal

Manual control and motion validation

Outcome

Full 6-joint manual operation

Requirements & Constraints

  • Achieve independent manual control of six joints
  • Build a functional arm from FDM-printed PLA components, hobby servos, and low-cost embedded hardware
  • Validate joint clearance, alignment, wiring, and power behavior before adding autonomy

Mechanical Design

  • Designed a 6 DOF arm with base yaw, shoulder, elbow, two wrist joints, and a gripper
  • Iterated the printed structure to improve fit, alignment, and joint clearance
  • Focused the first version on a buildable structure with usable reach and smooth joint motion

Electronics & Actuation

  • Used an ESP32 and dedicated servo driver to command each joint independently
  • Built the system on a breadboard so individual joints could be tested and debugged during integration
  • Added bulk capacitance and a power switch to stabilize the 6 V supply during servo current transients

Verification & V2 Inputs

Achieved full manual operation across all six joints, validating the arm structure, actuator selection, wiring approach, and power system. V1 also exposed packaging, wiring, clearance, and positioning issues that directly drove the smaller mechanical layout, cleaner electronics, and calibration process used in V2.

Project 01 | Version 02

V2 - Autonomous Pick and Place

STRYKER V2

Autonomous 6 DOF robotic arm that detects a target block, calculates a joint solution, and executes a tabletop pick-and-place sequence.

DOF

6 (J1 yaw, J2-J3 pitch, J4-J5 wrist, J6 gripper)

Controller

Arduino Nano + PCA9685 PWM driver

Vision

Overhead iPhone + workspace markers

Software

Python / OpenCV pipeline

Calibration

Camera mapping + measured servo calibration

Outcome

Completed three consecutive autonomous pick-and-place cycles

Requirements & Constraints

  • Detect a red block within a fixed tabletop workspace and transfer it without operator input
  • Use an overhead camera, hobby servos, and a 3D-printed mechanical structure
  • Improve the packaging, wiring, and repeatability issues identified in V1
  • Keep the arm, camera, controller, and power system compact enough for one workstation

Mechanical Redesign

  • Shortened the links and reorganized the arm layout to reduce bulk and improve motion
  • Evaluated reach, balance, and tipping risk before fabrication
  • Built a more compact structure while preserving coverage of the required workspace

Computer Vision Pipeline

  • Used an overhead iPhone camera and four workspace markers to map image coordinates to positions on the table
  • Used OpenCV to identify the red block and generate a pickup target
  • Implemented geometric inverse kinematics to convert the target position into joint commands

Electronics

  • Designed a custom carrier board integrating an Arduino Nano, PCA9685 PWM driver, and six servo signal connections
  • Replaced breadboard wiring with a cleaner, more serviceable control layout

Calibration & Motion Control

  • Measured real arm positions and calibrated servo commands to reduce mismatch between the kinematic model and the physical arm
  • Smoothed joint motion between positions to reduce shaking during transfer

Verification

  • Completed three consecutive autonomous pick-and-place cycles in the defined workstation
  • Each cycle detected the block, approached the pickup location, grasped it, transferred it to the drop zone, and returned clear

Remaining Limitation

Repeatability was limited by camera-to-table calibration and servo-position error. A future revision would use visual feedback during motion to correct target position in real time.

Project 02 - Controls and Mechatronics

SENTINEL

Self-balancing robot that uses an omni-wheel drivetrain, inertial sensing, and closed-loop control to remain upright on a ball.

Platform

Basketball + omni wheels

Power

12V battery, 2A motor drivers

Sensing

IMU (gyro + accel), encoders

Control

Balance-first PID control

Comms

Bluetooth controller interface

Outcome

Self-balanced for 64 seconds

Requirements & Constraints

  • Maintain upright balance on an unstable spherical platform
  • React quickly enough to move the ball under the robot as it tilts
  • Integrate drivetrain, sensors, power electronics, and real-time control using available motors, encoders, IMU sensing, and a 12 V battery system

Motor Sizing & Mechanical Design

  • Estimated the torque required to correct tilt and selected the motor and gearbox combination around that load
  • Designed the motor mount and omni-wheel layout to transfer wheel motion into controlled ball motion

Controls & Sensing

  • Used an IMU to measure tilt and angular motion and encoders to measure wheel rotation
  • Developed a motion model linking tilt error to wheel response
  • Implemented balance and steering control loops, prioritizing balance as the first functional milestone

Sensors, Control & Logging

  • Used Bluetooth for commands and re-zeroing during tests
  • Logged data over UART to tune from measured system behavior

Balance Controller

  • Tuned PID gains through repeated live testing
  • Filtered IMU data to reduce noise-driven controller response

Result

Successfully balanced upright for 64 seconds. Balancing was demonstrated; steering while maintaining stable balance was the next system-level capability to complete.

Project 05 - Embedded Systems

RANGER

Low-cost autonomous mobile robot that estimates position, avoids obstacles, detects a bright-orange target, and records detections on a simple 2D map.

Main Controller

BeagleBone Blue

Sensor MCU

Arduino Nano

Drive

Differential, DC motors w/ encoders

Sensors

Ultrasonic sonar, USB camera, thermopile

Perception

HSV blob tracking + thermal thresholding

Outcome

Obstacle avoidance, orange target approach, 2D mapping

Requirements & Constraints

  • Navigate autonomously using differential drive and estimate position from encoder and IMU feedback
  • Avoid obstacles using low-cost range sensors
  • Detect a bright-orange target using onboard vision and record detections on a 2D map
  • Integrate sensing, control, and mapping across a BeagleBone Blue and Arduino Nano

Odometry & Closed-Loop Motion

  • Used wheel encoder counts and IMU heading to estimate robot position during motion
  • Ran repeated square-path trials and compared final pose with ruler measurements to evaluate drift
  • Used waypoint logic: turn toward the next point, then drive the required distance
  • Added heading and distance feedback so the robot could correct while moving instead of relying only on dead reckoning

Obstacle Avoidance

  • Used right, front, and left ultrasonic sensors to monitor clearance
  • Defined stop and clear thresholds and used obstacle-avoidance routines to navigate around obstructions before returning to the route

Thermal Sensing & Signal Conditioning

  • Built an analog front end for the thermopile sensor and averaged ADC measurements to reduce signal variation
  • Used a 1.2 V threshold to classify hot versus not-hot conditions

Vision & Mapping

  • Used a USB camera and HSV color filtering to detect a bright-orange target
  • Used blob size and image position to determine whether the target was visible, centered, or close
  • Applied a 15-pixel blob threshold and re-centering behavior before forward motion, then stored detections in a simple 2D grid map

Verification

Demonstrated obstacle avoidance, target approach, and 2D detection mapping in the integrated system. Square-path trials identified odometry drift and showed where feedback control was necessary.

Project 04 - Controls and Embedded

LEONARDO

Two-axis drawing robot that converts images into motion commands and reproduces them on paper for approximately $200.

Controller

Arduino Nano 33 IoT w/ Motor Carrier

Actuation

2 DC motors w/ encoders + 1 servo

Build Cost

~$200

Output

Mona Lisa, Michigan M, US map, and custom images

Best Runtime

Michigan M in about 8 minutes

Requirements & Constraints

  • Convert custom image inputs into physical drawings
  • Keep motor torque within a defined operating range
  • Work within the reachable and controllable region of the mechanism
  • Improve drawing runtime without reducing output quality

Workspace & Torque Analysis

  • Mapped the robot workspace in MATLAB and excluded regions that could not be reached cleanly
  • Used torque analysis to constrain drawing paths to regions below 35 percent of stall torque
  • Defined an optimal drawing region that balanced reachability, motor loading, and drawing quality

Software Architecture

  • Converted image pixels into line segments, then converted those segments into joint commands through inverse kinematics and encoder feedback
  • Used two DC motors with encoders for motion and a servo to control pen position
  • Replaced the original left-to-right scan order with a nearest-segment search to reduce non-drawing travel

Result

Demonstrated drawings of the Mona Lisa, Michigan M, U.S. map, and other custom images. The path-planning update reduced the Michigan M drawing time from about 10 minutes to about 8 minutes at the same observed quality, saving roughly 0.5 to 1 second per segment.

Project 06 - Embedded Systems

PATHFINDER

One-week embedded-controls project demonstrating reliable line following with low-cost sensors, encoder feedback, and a cascaded control system.

Platform

MBot (differential drive)

Microcontroller

Raspberry Pi Pico (MicroPython)

Line Sensing

7-ch IR reflectance array (I2C)

Feedback

Quadrature encoders, 48:1 gear ratio

Control

Bang-bang to PID cascade

Smoothing

Exponential filter (alpha about 0.75)

Timeline

Built in 1 week

Outcome

Reliable full-course completion

Requirements & Constraints

  • Complete the defined line-following course autonomously
  • Build and validate the system within one week
  • Use a Raspberry Pi Pico, low-cost reflectance sensing, and differential drive
  • Maintain stable wheel speed and line tracking despite sensor noise and motor-current transients

Motor Control

  • Used interrupt-driven quadrature decoding in MicroPython to capture wheel motion without polling delays
  • Drove the motors using 10 kHz PWM and verified wheel-speed behavior on an oscilloscope

PID Wheel Speed Controller

  • Built a discrete PID wheel-speed controller from logged speed and current data
  • Added saturation handling to prevent integral windup

Line Sensor (I2C / PCA9557)

  • Used a 7-channel IR reflectance array with a PCA9557 I/O expander over I2C
  • Set sensor height to maintain reliable line detection while avoiding contact with the floor

Line Guidance & Verification

  • Used line position to choose left or right correction, then passed the command to the wheel-speed control loop
  • Used exponential smoothing with alpha about 0.75 to reduce in-rush current by about 150 mA
  • Ran the system with a 50 ms control loop and completed the course reliably within the one-week development window

Project 03 - Mechanical and Manufacturing

FORKRUNNER

Machined aluminum mobile robot with an integrated lead-screw forklift, designed for incline climbing, lifting, and object transport.

Gear Reduction

344:1 (verified safety margin)

Incline Rating

25 degrees worst-case traversal

Lift

Lead-screw, empirically characterized

Fabrication

Manual mill and lathe from stock aluminum

Outcome

Climbed incline and transported objects

Requirements & Constraints

  • Traverse a 25 degree worst-case incline
  • Lift and transport objects with a controlled vertical lift mechanism
  • Manufacture most structural parts from raw aluminum stock using a manual mill and lathe
  • Maintain chassis alignment and stiffness while keeping parts practical to machine

Drivetrain Analysis & Chassis Design

  • Calculated the drivetrain torque required for the 25 degree incline load case
  • Selected a 344:1 gear reduction to retain torque margin during incline climbing
  • Designed the chassis around drivetrain alignment, stiffness, manufacturability, and assembly access
  • Created CAD and part drawings before machining the aluminum components

Lift Mechanism & Verification

  • Selected a lead-screw lift for controlled vertical motion and mechanical advantage
  • Sized the lift around payload force and carriage mass and used guided vertical motion to reduce binding
  • Machined the chassis components from aluminum stock and integrated the drive system, lift carriage, and fork mechanism
  • Demonstrated incline climbing and object transport with the completed robot
